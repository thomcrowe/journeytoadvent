## Day 1 · Sunday, November 29

### Lo! He Comes with Clouds Descending

> **1** Lo! he comes with clouds descending,
> once for favored sinners slain;
> thousand thousand saints attending
> swell the triumph of his train:
> *Alleluia! Alleluia!*
> God appears on earth to reign.
>
> **3** Those dear tokens of his passion
> still his dazzling body bears,
> cause of endless exultation
> to his ransomed worshipers.
>
> — John Cennick (1752), altered by Charles Wesley (1758) · Tune: Helmsley

**Read:** Mark 13:24–37

**Reflect**

Advent does not begin in Bethlehem. It begins at the end of the world.

This catches people off guard every year. We come to church on the first Sunday with the wreath ready and the first purple candle waiting to be lit. We expect the long road to the manger to begin. Instead the Gospel gives us a darkened sun, falling stars, and the Son of Man coming in clouds. Then Jesus sums it all up in one word: *Watch.*

Charles Wesley's hymn takes him at his word. It starts where the Gospel ends, with the clouds, and doesn't flinch. If the hymn stopped there, it might frighten us. But a later verse turns the whole thing around. The Christ who comes in glory still bears "those dear tokens of his passion." He comes back with the wounds.

That is the heart of it. The judge of the living and the dead is not a stranger. When the clouds part, we will meet the face we already know from the cross, the hands we already know from the breaking of the bread.

**From the tradition**

> “We preach not one advent only of Christ, but a second also, far more glorious than the former.”
> — St. Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catechetical Lectures* 15

**Carry this:** If the one who comes to judge the world bears the marks of his love for you, what would it look like to watch for him this week without fear?

**Pray:** Almighty God, give us grace to cast away the works of darkness, and put on the armor of light, now in the time of this mortal life in which your Son Jesus Christ came to visit us in great humility; that in the last day, when he shall come again in his glorious majesty to judge both the living and the dead, we may rise to the life immortal; through him who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, now and for ever. *Amen.*
